\addchap*{Abstract}
Text-to-image (T2I) models can produce visually convincing images from short
natural-language prompts, but visual plausibility does not imply factual
knowledge. This thesis presents a benchmark for measuring the factual knowledge
of T2I models about real-world entities. The benchmark contains curated entities
from four domains and randomly sampled entities divided into Famous, Medium, and
Long-tail popularity tiers. Each entity is rendered from the same zero-context
prompt, ``An image of [entity].'' Images are evaluated with a blind visual
question-answering pipeline. The evaluation measures whether the model depicts
the correct entity type, whether it includes a distinctive descriptive feature,
and how many atomic visual propositions are hallucinated when compared with a
Wikipedia lead summary.

Three image-generation models are compared: GPT Image 2, FLUX.2-dev, and
FLUX.2-klein-4B. The final dataset contains 885 successful evaluations. On the
common set of 289 entities, GPT Image 2 reaches the lowest overall hallucination
rate, while FLUX.2-klein-4B produces the highest rate. For curated entities, the
best type-match rate is obtained by FLUX.2-dev, whereas GPT Image 2 performs best
on distinctive feature presence. Hallucination rates vary strongly by popularity
tier, with Long-tail entities generally producing fewer visual propositions and
more unsupported facts. The results show that T2I models contain measurable,
domain-dependent factual knowledge, but that their outputs should not be treated
as reliable factual representations without explicit verification.
